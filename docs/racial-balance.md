# Racial Balance

## Strategic Framework

Race choice is the most consequential strategic decision in character creation. Three viable player strategies exist:

1. **Generalist fillers** - adequate at everything, moderate lifespan, easy to replace
2. **Short-lived sprinters** - fastest at one role, peak quickly, age out
3. **Long-lived investments** - slower to start, hit max level, decades/centuries of service at peak

Every race must have at least one compelling reason to choose it. If a race cannot justify its existence through some combination of XP specialty, resistances, abilities, or lifespan, it should be cut.

## Race Roster

### Short-Lived Races

#### Human - Generalist Filler

- **Identity**: Jack of all trades. All four basic classes at 1.0 modifier. No resistances, no abilities, no weaknesses. Moderate lifespan.
- **Why pick**: Fill any role adequately. The default when you need a body in a slot and don't want to think about it. Easy to replace when they age out.
- **Strategic role**: Early roster padding. Transition to specialists and investments as the guild grows.

#### Lizman - Sprint Fighter

- **Identity**: Fastest Fighter in the game. Burns bright, ages out fast.
- **Why pick**: When you need a frontline tank NOW. Reaches combat milestones 1+ levels ahead of any other Fighter. Cold-blooded warriors built for physical dominance.
- **Strategic role**: Early-game tank that carries the party through dangerous first floors, then retires as a Dwarf Fighter catches up and surpasses.
- **Proposed change**: Fighter modifier 0.8 -> 0.7

#### Rawulf - Sprint Priest

- **Identity**: Fastest Priest in the game. Gets the party healed and alive through early floors.
- **Why pick**: When you need group heals and resurrection access ASAP. Reaches healing spell tiers before any other Priest.
- **Strategic role**: Early-game healer that keeps the party alive, then passes the torch to a Gnome or Elf Priest.
- **Proposed change**: Priest modifier 0.9 -> 0.7
- **Missing resistance**: Cold resistance (from Wizardry source, not yet implemented)

#### Felpurr - Sprint Thief

- **Identity**: Fastest Thief in the game. Gets traps disarmed and locks picked at the earliest opportunity.
- **Why pick**: When you need trap/lock skills maxed quickly. Cat-like reflexes translate to early rogue dominance.
- **Strategic role**: Early-game rogue that handles dungeon hazards, then yields to a Hobbit Thief for the long haul.
- **Proposed change**: Thief modifier 0.9 -> 0.7
- **Missing resistance**: Cold resistance (from Wizardry source, not yet implemented)

#### Mook - Sprint Ranger

- **Identity**: Fastest Ranger in the game. Large, intelligent, keen-sensed tracker/scout.
- **Why pick**: When you need a hybrid physical/caster that peaks quickly. Mooks have the STR and INT to support Ranger's dual demands. Their size and senses make them natural scouts.
- **Strategic role**: Early-game hybrid that provides both physical and magical support, then ages out as an Elf Ranger takes over.
- **Proposed change**: Ranger modifier 1.1 -> 0.7
- **Missing resistance**: Cold resistance (from Wizardry source, not yet implemented)
- **Note**: Mook stat spread (STR 10-20, INT 10-20, VIT 10-20) naturally supports Ranger requirements (STR 10, PIE 10, VIT 10). PIE 6-16 requires some bonus point investment but is achievable.

### Long-Lived Races

#### Elf - Eternal Investment

- **Identity**: The ultimate long-term companion. Best caster lifespan in the game by a wide margin (325yr prime). Watches generations of shorter-lived companions come and go.
- **Why pick**: When you want a character who will be with you for the entire game and beyond. Mage/Priest at 0.9 means slower than Faerie or Gnome at those roles, but centuries of service at L20 more than compensates.
- **Strategic role**: The anchor. Build your guild around Elves and they'll outlast everything.
- **Resistance**: Sleep (75%)

#### Dwarf - Long-Lived Tank

- **Identity**: Permanent frontline Fighter. Tough, durable, 130 years of service at peak.
- **Why pick**: When you want a tank that never needs replacing. Fighter 0.9 is slower than Lizman, but a Dwarf Fighter at L20 will still be swinging when the Lizman's grandchildren have retired.
- **Strategic role**: The Lizman replacement. Invest early, reap rewards for over a century.
- **Resistance**: Poison (75%)

#### Gnome - Long-Lived Healer

- **Identity**: Best sustained Priest in the game. 140 years of healing at peak.
- **Why pick**: Priest 0.8 means fast leveling AND long life. Only outpaced by Rawulf's proposed 0.7 in the early game. Once both hit mid-levels, the Gnome keeps going for a century+.
- **Strategic role**: The Rawulf replacement. Your permanent healer.
- **Note**: No resistances. Gnome's value is purely in the Priest 0.8 + lifespan combination. Consider whether Gnome needs a resistance or ability to round out their identity.

#### Hobbit - Long-Lived Rogue

- **Identity**: Best sustained Thief in the game. 50 years of prime - shortest among long-lived races but still dwarfs Felpurr's 23 years.
- **Why pick**: Thief 0.8 means fast leveling. Paralysis + Stone immunity makes them extremely durable against the nastiest status effects. The combination of Thief speed + defensive immunities is powerful.
- **Strategic role**: The Felpurr replacement. A rogue who can survive encounters that would petrify others.
- **Resistance**: Paralysis (75%), Petrification (75%)

#### Faerie - Long-Lived Caster

- **Identity**: Fastest Mage in the game (0.8) AND 125 years of prime. No short-lived Mage sprinter exists, making Faerie uniquely dominant at the Mage role.
- **Why pick**: Best Mage modifier in the game with a long shelf life. Also decent at Bard (0.9) and Psionic (0.9). Fragile (low STR/VIT) but devastatingly fast at magic.
- **Strategic role**: Your primary caster. No competition from a short-lived sprinter means Faerie is the clear choice for Mage from day one.
- **Note**: No resistances. Wizardry Gaiden IV gave Faeries the ability to equip heavy gear - consider whether a different ability or resistance would help here.

#### Dracon - Breath Weapon Utility

- **Identity**: The only race with a unique combat ability. Acid breath deals half current HP as damage to an enemy group, once per battle, no MP cost. 80 years of prime.
- **Why pick**: The breath weapon alone justifies the pick. At early levels when MP is scarce, a free AoE attack is a game-changer. Even at high levels, it's a reliable opening salvo. Any class benefits from having it.
- **Strategic role**: Versatile slot-filler whose breath weapon adds AoE to any party composition. Fighter 0.9 makes them decent tanks too. The utility is class-independent.
- **Resistance**: Paralysis (75%), Petrification (75%)
- **Breath weapon spec**: Acid damage equal to half Dracon's current HP. Targets a single enemy at low levels, expanding to full enemy group at level 9+. Once per battle. No MP cost.

## XP Modifier Changes

### Proposed Changes

| Race | Class | Current | Proposed | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lizman | Fighter | 0.8 | 0.7 | Widen gap as fastest Fighter, reinforce sprinter identity |
| Rawulf | Priest | 0.9 | 0.7 | Become fastest Priest (currently Gnome 0.8 is faster AND longer-lived) |
| Felpurr | Thief | 0.9 | 0.7 | Become fastest Thief (currently Hobbit 0.8 is faster AND longer-lived) |
| Mook | Ranger | 1.1 | 0.7 | Give Mook a unique sprint niche (currently a worse Human) |

### Speed Champions Per Class (After Changes)

| Class | Short-Lived Sprinter | Mod | Long-Lived Investment | Mod |

Mage is the only basic/hybrid class without a short-lived sprinter. This makes Faerie's position uniquely strong.

### Early Career Impact (First 20 Years)

With proposed 0.7 modifiers, sprinters gain a consistent 1-level advantage over equivalent long-lived races at every milestone:

| Career Year | Sprinter (0.7) | Standard (0.8) | Slow (1.0) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | L4 | L3 | L3 |
| 10 | L6 | L5 | L5 |
| 15 | L8 | L7 | L7 |
| 20 | L10 | L10 | L9 |

One level earlier means earlier spell tier access, earlier stat thresholds for class changes, and more HP/MP when hitting dangerous floors.

## Resistances and Abilities

### Target Implementation

All racial resistances should be 75% chance to resist (strong but not immunity). drpg-gd currently implements the status resistances as full immunities - these need to be changed to 75%.

| Race | Status Resistances (75%) | Elemental Resistances (50%) |
|---|---|---|
| Elf | Sleep | - |
| Dwarf | Poison | - |
| Hobbit | Paralysis, Petrification | - |
| Dracon | Paralysis, Petrification | - |
| Rawulf | - | Cold |
| Mook | - | Cold |
| Felpurr | - | Cold |
| Human | - | - |
| Gnome | - | - |
| Faerie | - | - |
| Lizman | - | - |

Cold resistance for Rawulf, Mook, and Felpurr exists in the Wizardry Gaiden IV source material but was never implemented in drpg2 or drpg-gd. It should be added at 50% (elemental resistances are weaker than status resistances).

### Missing Abilities

| Race | Missing Ability | Spec |
|---|---|---|
| Dracon | Acid Breath | Half current HP as acid damage to enemy group, once per battle, no MP cost. Single target at low level, group target at L9+. |

### Races With No Resistances or Abilities

| Race | Compensating Strengths |
|---|---|
| Human | 1.0 across all basic classes (ultimate generalist), moderate lifespan |
| Gnome | Priest 0.8 (fastest long-lived Priest), 140yr prime |
| Faerie | Mage 0.8 (fastest Mage in game, no short-lived competitor), 125yr prime |
| Lizman | Fighter 0.7 proposed (fastest Fighter in game), high STR/VIT stat spread |

These four races justify themselves through XP modifiers and stat spreads alone. No resistances needed.

## Aging Thresholds

Life phase thresholds are derived from two inputs per race (start age, max age) plus universal percentage breakpoints applied to career length (max_age - start_age):

- **Youth**: 0% to 7% of career
- **Prime**: 7% to 50% of career
- **Decline**: 50% to 80% of career
- **Fragile**: 80% to 100% of career (death from old age begins)

| Human | 18 | 80 | drpg2 / real-world baseline |
| Elf | 75 | 650 | drpg2 / classic fantasy elf |

| Gnome | 60 | 350 | drpg2 / slightly longer than dwarf |
| Hobbit | 30 | 120 | drpg2 / Tolkien halflings |

## Generational Overlap

Long-lived races outlive multiple generations of short-lived companions. This has deep narrative implications for the relationship and event systems.

| Long-Lived Race | Career (yrs) | Human Generations | Rawulf Generations |
|---|---|---|---|
| Elf | 325 | 12.0x | 17.1x |
| Gnome | 140 | 5.2x | 7.4x |
| Dwarf | 130 | 4.8x | 6.8x |
| Faerie | 125 | 4.6x | 6.6x |
| Dracon | 80 | 3.0x | 4.2x |
| Hobbit | 50 | 1.9x | 2.6x |

An Elf Mage will genuinely watch 12+ generations of Human companions and 17+ generations of Rawulf companions come, grow, peak, decline, and die. This is not flavor text - it is mechanical reality that the narrative systems should reflect through marks, relationships, and events.

## Balance Modeling Tool

The Python script `tools/age_balance_plotter.py` simulates career progression with an accelerating XP model based on actual monster data. Run with:

```
uv run --with matplotlib --with numpy python3 tools/age_balance_plotter.py
```

Key simulation parameters:
- 100 encounters per year
- 1.5 years to clear a floor
- Party size of 6 (XP split)
- XP per encounter scales by floor depth (25 XP on floor 1 up to 20000 XP on floor 20)
