# Dungeon sets: the shape of the story

A proposal for how the game's story is structured: several procedurally generated dungeons, a final one, an ending, and free play after. It builds on [deep-time.md](deep-time.md), which made aging the cost of going deep, and on the racial strategies in [racial-balance.md](racial-balance.md).

## The shape

The story is a **set of four dungeons** and a **fifth**. Each dungeon is generated fresh and goes down to a master. Defeating the master collapses that dungeon for good. When all four have collapsed, a condensed fifth dungeon appears. Defeating its master ends the story and shows the ending. After that the game goes on in free play: a new set of four appears, and the cycle starts again.

There are no campaigns with gaps between them. Time is continuous, and the unit of play is the dungeon run. The roster works the way it does in Wizardry: party members are interchangeable. Whoever is in the party goes down. Everyone else stays in town, working a job, training, earning a little, and aging slowly on the surface calendar.

## Characters and dungeons

An average character lasts through two to four dungeons before age takes them, and that number varies a lot by race:

- **Short-lived races** (Rawulf, Mook, Lizman, Felpurr) level fastest in their best classes and peak early. They might see two dungeons.
- **Humans** see two or three.
- **Long-lived races** (Hobbit, Faerie, Dracon, Dwarf, Gnome, Elf) start slower but can outlive everyone and keep getting stronger the whole time. An elf could see the whole story and go well into free play, but only if the dungeon doesn't kill them first.

The story takes five dungeons, so most of the guild turns over before it ends. That's the race trade-off at character creation. Sprinters get you through the early dungeons fast, while long-lived characters, kept alive, carry the guild through the end and outperform anyone who came and went.

Already done in support of this:

- **Levels are uncapped.** Past 20, each level costs a flat 68,000 XP before race and class modifiers, as in Wizardry.
- **The Youth XP bonus is gone.** It lasted 7% of a race's lifespan, which favored long-lived races. Short-lived races keep their speed through the race and class XP modifiers.
- **Raising by spell is a gamble.** A failed Resurrect leaves ashes, and a failed raise from ashes loses the character for good. The temple always raises a body.

## Gold and the guild bank

Each character carries their own purse, as in Wizardry. The guild also keeps a **bank**, a communal store of gold and items that anyone can draw on in town. Today the party shares one pool of gold, so this replaces that.

## Collapse

When a dungeon's master falls, the dungeon collapses. The party gets out and the dungeon is gone from the town's list. Its maps, seals and remaining loot go with it.

## Masters and art

Each dungeon needs its own master. Until more art arrives, masters use placeholder art or tinted copies of existing monsters. The same goes for any monster tiers in later sets.

## Aging by depth

Deep time stays as built: every step below ages the party according to the floor it's on, and deeper floors cost far more. Measuring what that adds up to over a dungeon run is the next piece of work, and its results belong here.

## Open questions

- **Order and difficulty of the four.** Are all four open at once, so the player picks the order, or do they open one after another? If they're open together, do they differ in difficulty and depth, or does difficulty rise with how many have collapsed?
- **What "condensed" means for the fifth.** Fewer floors, starting deep, would make it short and dangerous, and very expensive in years under deep time.
- **Later sets in free play.** Presumably each new set is harder than the last, since the surviving veterans keep their levels.
- **Leaving a collapsing dungeon.** Does the party walk out, or is it thrown back to town? And what happens to bodies left below when it collapses?

## Build order

1. **Collapse and the set.** Several dungeons at once, collapse on a master's defeat, the fifth dungeon, the story ending, and a new set after. Masters start as placeholders or tinted copies.
2. **Purses and the bank.** Per-character gold and the guild bank.
3. **Later sets.** Tiers for free play.
4. **The chronicle.** The saga covers every dungeon so far, and the guild hall keeps a record of collapsed dungeons and Legends.
